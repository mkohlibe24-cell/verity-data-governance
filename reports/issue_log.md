## Root-cause analyses (5 Whys) for source-data issues

### ISS-0011: DQ-C09 failed: Every business day has transaction volume

**Root cause:** The source has no transactions at all on 29 February 2020, so the leap day is missing from the extract.

1. Why did the daily-volume rule fail? One day in the period has 0 records: 2020-02-29.
2. Why is only that day empty? Every other day has thousands of records, so it is not a quiet day but a gap.
3. Why would a single date be missing? 2020 is a leap year and 29 February is the only date that exists in it but not in 2019.
4. Why was the leap day skipped? The generating/extract process most likely builds its calendar without leap-day handling.
5. Why was it not caught upstream? There was no completeness check on daily volume before this pipeline.

**Remediation:** Re-run the load for the affected days; add file-arrival monitoring.

### ISS-0020: DQ-A01 failed: Cardholder is an adult at transaction date

**Root cause:** The feed mixes supplementary (minor) cardholders with primary cardholders and carries no relationship type.

1. Why are there cardholders under 18? Their date of birth puts them at 14-17 years old.
2. Why would minors hold cards? Issuers offer supplementary cards to teenagers on a parent's account.
3. Why does that look like an error? The feed has no field saying whether a card is primary or supplementary.
4. Why no field? The extract was designed for transactions, not customer relationships.
5. Why does it matter? Age-based analytics and KYC checks treat every card as a primary account.

**Remediation:** Confirm card relationship type (primary vs supplementary) and add it to the feed.

### ISS-0021: DQ-V06 failed: ZIP code is 5 digits

**Root cause:** ZIP code is typed as a number in the source extract, so leading zeros are stripped.

1. Why do ZIPs fail the 5-digit format rule? Many have 4 digits (e.g. 1257 instead of 01257).
2. Why 4 digits? The leading zero is missing for New England ZIPs that start with 0.
3. Why is it missing? The extract writes ZIP as an integer, and integers drop leading zeros.
4. Why an integer? The source schema defines ZIP as numeric, not as a text code.
5. Why was that allowed? There is no data standard saying identifiers/codes must be stored as text.

**Remediation:** Store ZIP as text at source. Interim fix applied in silver (left-pad to 5 digits).

### ISS-0022: DQ-V11 failed: Card number has a valid length (12-19 digits)

**Root cause:** Card numbers are stored as integers upstream, so a leading zero is lost for some cards.

1. Why do some cards have 11 digits? Valid card numbers have 12-19 digits.
2. Why 11? Their first digit was 0 and it was dropped.
3. Why dropped? The source stores the card number as a number instead of text.
4. Why does it still pass Luhn? Removing a leading zero does not change the Luhn checksum.
5. Why was it allowed? No length check on card numbers at capture.

**Remediation:** Store card numbers as text end to end; confirm affected cards with the issuer system.

### ISS-0023: DQ-K01 failed: unix_time agrees with the transaction timestamp

**Root cause:** unix_time is populated from a different epoch than the transaction timestamp (offset of about 7 years).

1. Why do timestamps disagree? unix_time is about 2,556 days behind trans_date_trans_time on every record.
2. Why every record? The offset is systematic, not random, so it comes from how the field is generated.
3. Why generated differently? The simulator derives unix_time from a different base date than the timestamp.
4. Why was it not caught? No consistency check existed between the two time fields.
5. Why no check? The field had no documented owner or definition in a data dictionary.

**Remediation:** Treat trans_date_trans_time as authoritative; unix_time excluded from gold; ask the source owner to fix or retire the field.

### ISS-0024: DQ-K04 failed: Each merchant maps to one category

**Root cause:** Merchant names are used as keys but are not unique; the feed has no merchant ID.

1. Why does a merchant have two categories? Two different merchants share the same name.
2. Why are they merged? The pipeline can only identify merchants by name.
3. Why only by name? The source does not include the acquirer's merchant ID.
4. Why not? The extract was built before merchant-level analytics were needed.
5. Why does it matter? Category-based rules (TM-08) and merchant reporting use the wrong category for some rows.

**Remediation:** Request the acquirer merchant ID in the feed and use it as the merchant key.
